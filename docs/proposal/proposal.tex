\section{Motivation}
The NAS Unseen-Data Challenge (AutoML 2026) evaluates a fully automated
pipeline on \emph{hidden} classification datasets under a strict
wall-clock budget per dataset. All inputs arrive as image-shaped tensors,
but they are semantically heterogeneous: besides natural and satellite
images, several datasets are \emph{encoded} data, such as rendered text,
Sudoku boards, or chess positions, where the spatial prior of a CNN is
misleading. All decisions must be made at runtime, blind.

Our starting point is the winning method of the 2025 edition, EGNAS
\cite{egnas}, a macro--micro search (depth, width, per-layer operation)
over convolutional blocks. Reproducing it surfaced two systematic
weaknesses. First, rankings from short training runs are unreliable:
operations that lead after a few epochs often lose after full training,
so fine-grained (micro) operation search tends to \emph{harm} final
accuracy. Second, the space is confined to one architecture family: in
our preliminary experiments a \emph{fixed} 2-D dilated-convolution
backbone in the style of temporal convolutional networks \cite{tcn}
clearly outperformed the fully searched CNN baseline on most datasets,
while plain CNNs and randomly sampled wide-vocabulary architectures won
precisely on the encoded datasets, mirroring the organisers' observation
that random search can beat directed NAS there \cite{geada}. The leverage
lies in choosing the right architecture \emph{family} per dataset, not in
fine-grained operation search.

\section{Research Questions}
\textbf{RQ1.} Can macro--micro NAS \cite{egnas} be generalised across
heterogeneous architecture families (plain CNNs, 2-D dilated and 1-D
temporal convolution networks), making families \emph{comparable} within a
single time-budgeted search stage despite unreliable short-training
rankings?
\textbf{RQ2.} Can cheap, training-free measurements of the raw data decide
at runtime which architectural prior fits an unseen dataset?
\textbf{RQ3.} How can harmful data augmentation be detected at runtime,
given that augmentation acts as regularisation (it costs accuracy early
and pays late) and that, as we observe, augmentation decisions do not
transfer between architecture families?

\section{Approach}
We propose a \emph{measure--gate--train} pipeline (figure on page~2).
(1)~\emph{Measure}: a fingerprint computed directly from the raw input
tensors, with no training involved. The mean correlation of neighbouring
pixels separates encoded from image-like data with a wide margin
(Table~\ref{tab:fp}). (2)~\emph{Macro stage}: for image-like data the
dilated backbone is selected directly. Only encoded data pays for a
bake-off in which all families are trained briefly at matched wall-clock.
The leader is adopted only if it beats the robust default by more than
the measured ranking noise, otherwise the pipeline \emph{abstains} to the
default, which makes every decision bounded-downside.
(3)~\emph{Augmentation probe}: a two-arm A/B test run \emph{after} the
family choice, with arms of the chosen family, using an asymmetric rule
that deviates from the default only on a clear margin (RQ3).
(4)~\emph{Micro stage}: depth search anchored on an incumbent default. A
deviation must win significantly \emph{and} survive verification by
re-training at a longer horizon. (5)~A \emph{random-search arm} over a
wide operation vocabulary runs only on encoded data and only if budget
remains \cite{geada}. (6)~\emph{Train to budget}: the winner trains on
the full remaining time with a cosine learning-rate schedule sized to
reach zero exactly when the budget ends. All stage budgets are fractions
of the clock, so the pipeline degrades gracefully from many hours down to
minutes (the evaluation budget is unknown).

\section{Required Steps}
(1)~Reproduce the EGNAS baseline and probe candidate architecture
families at full budget (done). (2)~Validate each decision
mechanism in isolation by replaying it against known full-budget outcomes
(gate reliability, fingerprint separation, augmentation transfer).
(3)~Integrate the pipeline and verify all execution paths, including the
low-budget collapse behaviour. (4)~Full multi-seed evaluation.
(5)~A budget-ladder study (0.5h to 8h) measuring which decision stages pay
at which budget. (6)~A generalisation test on external datasets that
played no role in any design choice (CIFAR-10/100, EMNIST, MedMNIST,
spectrograms). (7)~Submission packaging and stress tests.

\section{Evaluation Strategy}
Primary evaluation uses the 13 public competition datasets with the
official adjusted score (accuracy versus a per-dataset benchmark, capped),
at least three seeds for final configurations. Baselines: the reproduced
EGNAS search, each fixed architecture family, and an oracle that always
picks the best family, bounding the achievable gain of the routing. Every
stage writes its measurements, margins, and choices to a per-dataset
decision record, so gains and losses are attributable to individual
decisions. Robustness is evaluated across budgets (30 minutes to 12 hours), on the
external suite, and against published per-dataset competition bests
\cite{geada}.
